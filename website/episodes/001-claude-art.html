EPISODE 001 / AI & SCIENCE

Claude spotted an unusual enzyme system.
What does that mean?

Rogue Signal · 25 September 2026 · Reviewed and approved by Barry Greaves

Watch on YouTube ↗

Anthropic says Claude identified previously unnoticed features of a system in bacteriophages—the viruses that infect bacteria. The team calls it ART: array-associated reverse transcriptases.

What is new?

The enzyme itself was already known. Anthropic credits Claude with noticing a nearby repeating DNA array and an accessory protein that define the system. [1]

Why mention CRISPR?

The repeat pattern resembles CRISPR arrays. Early laboratory work found that the array is expressed as short RNAs. That resemblance is a research clue; it does not establish a gene-editing capability. [1]

What remains unknown?

The system’s function is unknown. The findings were released as a preprint, without peer review. The Next Web reports that the researchers have not yet demonstrated the enzyme’s activity or shown that it acts on those RNAs. [2]

Our perspective

The meaningful distinction is between finding a promising research lead and proving what it can do. AI can help direct attention; experiments must establish the biology.
